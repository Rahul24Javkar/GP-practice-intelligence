Harmful effects are likely driven by modifiable behaviours — fragmented attention, poor sleep, sedentary lifestyle and reduced face-to-face socialising — rather than screens themselves. Prevention focuses on digital hygiene, protecting sleep, promoting physical activity and social and cognitive engagement, and managing cardiovascular and mental-health risk factors. Up to 45% of dementia cases may be delayed or prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, effective chronic disease management and reducing environmental risk factors. For GPs, this represents another opportunity to frame preventive healthcare not only as protecting the heart and metabolic health, but also as protecting the brain. Every discussion about exercise, smoking cessation, blood pressure, diabetes management or social connection may also be a conversation about reducing a patient's future risk of dementia.
